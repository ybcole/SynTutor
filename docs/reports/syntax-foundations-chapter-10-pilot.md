## Source receipt

Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. The [storyboard](../planning/syntax-foundations-chapter-10-storyboard.md) maps printed pp. 224–253 (PDF pp. 239–268) and records the figures checked. The pilot adapts their V10 analyses to original examples.

| Scope | Printed anchors and analysis |
| --- | --- |
| Four forms and tense | Pp. 225–229, [6], [11], [14a], [17], [21–22]. The bare infinitive and passive participle carry [-tense] on V in the examples. The to-infinitive has a feature-only branch above “to”. The -ing clause marks the first -ing verb. The passive participle comparison shows the object gap and its link to the subject. |
| Subjects and introductory positions | Pp. 224–225, [1–3], pp. 229–232, [26–33], and Discussion 1 on p. 244. A free subject has an unindexed dot. Nia and the controlled subject share index 1. | Clause functions | Pp. 232–236, [40–65], and Discussion 2 on p. 244. The same lexical content can appear in a clausal subject or a complement of P. A to-infinitive can give the purpose of an action. |
| Type I and Type II | Pp. 239–243, [98–102]. “Expected” has one clausal complement with an overt subject. “Persuaded” has an object NP and a clause with a silent subject controlled by that object. The transfer uses “advised” to check the same distinction. |
