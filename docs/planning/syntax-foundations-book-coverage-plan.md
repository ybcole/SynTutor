# Syntax Foundations book coverage and implementation plan

**Baseline** · `feat/lessons/revision-integration` at `e537d98`. This audit uses the revised Chapters 1–11, including the chapter introductions, diagrams, tree repair, transfer tasks, recaps, and opening and closing checks. The Cambridge companion is outside the Burton-Roberts sequence.

**Reference** · Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences: An Introduction to English Syntax*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. Page references below are printed pages. The audit compared the book’s contents and selected passages with the lesson modules, assessment overlays, and existing storyboards. Check each proposed example and figure against its cited pages while implementing it. **Coverage standard** · A concept is taught when the lesson explains it and shows it in a usable example. A definition or brief mention counts as partial coverage. Every substantive book section should have an original example, and every important distinction should give learners a chance to apply it. Longer derivations, disputed analyses, and appendix material can live in clearly named extension pages with worked feedback. This preserves the current short core route while making the full treatment available.

## Chapter-by-chapter comparison

The page ranges include the book’s exercises and discussions. The plan carries their distinct skills forward as original tasks with worked explanations. The book’s introductory reading guidance on pp. 1–5 can be linked from Chapter 1 as orientation. The further reading after Chapter 11 is a reference.

| Chapter and printed pages | Included now | Partial or missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Constituents, 6–23** | Nested groups, omission, replacement, question answers, flat and nested phrase markers, triangles, immediate dominance, and two readings of an attachment. | Movement is named in the recap but lacks a worked test. Context dependence is illustrated with a longer landmark, but learners get little practice deciding when the same string is a constituent in one sentence and crosses a boundary in another. Longer recursive drawing practice is missing. |
| **2 · Functions, 24–45** | Full subject and predicate spans, yes/no question test, NP and VP under S, category versus function, sisters, head, modifier, and complement. | The omission examples imply the difference between one-way modifier dependence and the head–complement relation, but do not give a complete comparison with arrows or a transfer on a nested phrase. Subject–predicate dependency needs its own explanation. Add a case where omission changes the verb use or reading. |
| **3 · Categories, 46–66** | Noun plural as a clue, noun versus NP, names and pronouns, A/AP and Adv/AdvP, degree words, headed PPs and locative `there`, PP coordination. | Noun diagnostics beyond plural, including noun uses where the plural clue fails, and adjective/adverb tests beyond the supplied examples need practice. Coordination stays mostly at the PP level. Lexical coordination, modifier scope over conjuncts, and limited cases of unlike categories are absent. |
| **4 · Basic VP, 67–86** | All six basic verb patterns, transitive and intransitive uses of the same verb, recipient NP and `to` PP, subject and object predicatives, and three uses of PPs. | Verb-form recognition, pronoun case as evidence for object function, and the book’s governance terminology are missing. The `for` recipient/beneficiary pattern lacks a worked contrast. The AP object predicative is shown, but comparable NP and PP cases and ambiguous two-NP complements need practice. |
| **5 · Adverbials, 87–110** | VP adjuncts, PP/AdvP/NP examples, basic VP versus one higher VP, `did so` replacement, two edge positions, particle placement, understood recipient marked E, event versus sentence scope, disjuncts and conjuncts. | No worked multi-adjunct tree showing a third VP layer and a scope contrast. Medial or displaced adjuncts need an explanation of the book’s diagram limits. One particle and one ellipsis case leave little practice with other particle patterns and context-dependent omissions. Sentence-level attachment beyond a clear edge position is brief. |
| **6 · Auxiliary VPs, 111–140** | MOD, PERF, PROG and PASS, stacked VPs, the tense carrier, basic form selection, adverbial scope, simple passive, `not`, do-support, yes/no fronting and lexical `be`. | Tense versus time needs its own treatment. Each auxiliary’s form and meaning needs fuller practice. Lexical `have` versus perfect `have` is missing, including the book’s two question and negation patterns for lexical `have`. Complex and ditransitive passives, auxiliary coordination, and passive questions with two distinct gaps are absent. |
| **7 · NP structure, 141–170** | DET and NOM, demonstratives and other basic determiner types, empty DET, possessive phrases, pre-determiners, AP layers, QA/PartP/compounds in brief, PP attachment, postposed APs and indefinite pronoun modification. | Nested possessives, detailed QA/PartP/compound examples, noun complement versus modifier, ambiguous PP stacks and partitive analyses need more treatment. The appendix on `one` as a substitute for NOM and its refined structure is missing. |
| **8 · Embedded clauses, 171–195** | Coordination versus embedding, three nested clauses, C/S/S′, overt `that`, empty C, `whether`, subject and extraposition, V/A/N/P complements, subject predicative and basic adverbial attachment. | Abbreviated clause analysis is implicit in the bracket exercises. Learners need practice naming the host and function of every clause. Interrogative `if` distribution, verbs that require extraposition, passive evidence for clausal direct objects, ambiguous temporal/causal `since`, and multiword clause introducers need coverage. |
| **9 · Wh-clauses, 196–223** | Recovering a gap from an answer, C2 versus C1, subject and object questions, PP stranding versus whole-PP fronting, main versus embedded interrogatives, a deeper gap in brackets, relative versus noun complement, relative `that` and omission, restrictive versus non-restrictive attachment. | AP and AdvP questions are briefly named. The inventory of wh-phrases and embedded question functions needs more practice. A full long-distance derivation, the `one` test applied to relatives, and restrictions on `that` and omission in non-restrictives need explicit treatment. Subject-question C1 alternatives belong in a source-labelled extension. |
| **10 · Non-finite clauses, 224–253** | Four non-finite forms and [-tense], overt/free/controlled subjects, indices, `for`/`whether`/wh positions, subject/adverbial/P complement, brief A/N complements and NP modification, Type I versus II, optional diagnostics with `there` and `promise`. | No worked non-finite relative tracking a silent wh-phrase separately from a silent subject or passive gap. Stacked non-finite auxiliaries, extraposition and its restrictions, the two A-complement patterns, and a fuller range of V-complement and control patterns are missing or brief. |
| **11 · Languages, sentences and grammars, 254–268** | Language variety in brief, toy finite list, unbounded recursive coordination, two groupings of the same sequence, rule applications, implicit knowledge and a mini grammar assigning structure and order. | The bridge from natural sentence embedding to unboundedness is brief. The book’s flat coordination option lacks a comparison. An extension should explore how structures and rules explain novel sentences across a stated variety, and include a fuller original grammar exercise. Historical discussion can be covered in a short reading note. |

## Implementation order and acceptance targets

Keep the contextual opening used by the revised lessons. Start with a question or short scene, show the example, then name the technical term. Use original sentences, keep the existing chapter notation, and put explanations directly under each tree. Every added tree should retain **Read tree as text** above **Tree notation**. Leave the more technical explanation available after the learner has tried the concrete example.

| Order | Placement and work | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Constituents** | Extend **Test a group in context** with a worked movement test and a near-miss where the same string crosses a boundary. Add an optional nested phrase-marker repair adapted to the reasoning on pp. 10–18 and 19–23. | Learner can state what moved as a unit, explain why the near-miss fails in the given context, and separate direct from indirect dominance. |
| **2 · Functions** | Extend **Read relations between sisters** with a dependency diagram beside the phrase marker. Compare the top subject–predicate pairing with one-way modification and the head–complement relation. Use an original pair that makes omission change the reading. Anchor pp. 24–38. | Learner can name the relation and point to evidence about what depends on what, while holding the intended reading fixed. |
| **3 · Categories** | Add a word-class comparison after **Find the noun and the whole phrase**. Extend **Join two prepositional phrases** with word versus phrase coordination and a two-reading modifier-scope example. Put source-specific unlike-category cases in a short extension. Anchor pp. 47–62. | Learner can justify a category with more than one clue and distinguish the size and scope of coordinate units. |
| **4 · Basic VP** | Add a short verb-form and pronoun-case comparison in **Find the verb and its complements**. Extend **Follow both objects** with a `for` comparison. Add NP and PP object predicatives and a two-NP comparison to **Find what the phrase describes**. Anchor pp. 67–79. | Learner distinguishes all six patterns in fresh sentences and can explain why two post-verbal NPs do not settle the pattern by themselves. |
| **5 · Adverbials** | Add a comparison with two adjuncts after **Trace and attach an adjunct**. Reuse `did so` to check spans and show VP₁–VP₃. Extend **Check what can move** with a medial-position note. Add one particle contrast, one understood-role contrast, and one more sentence-adverbial scope example. Anchor pp. 87–101. | Learner can explain the attachment of both adjuncts, apply the pronoun test to a fresh particle example, and identify what a whole-statement comment modifies. |
| **6 · Auxiliary VPs** | Add **Tense and time** before the chain. Expand the form comparison with plain, past-participle, and -ing forms. Add **Two uses of have and be** near questions. Show lexical `have` with do-support and note the variety-dependent direct inversion pattern. Add complex and ditransitive passives and a two-gap passive question after the simple passive. Treat coordination as extended practice. Anchor pp. 112–134. | Learner can locate tense while distinguishing time reference, classify lexical and auxiliary uses, and trace each gap to its own overt element in a passive question. |
| **7 · NP structure** | Extend DET with nested possession and make QA, PartP, and compound examples traceable. Add complement versus modifier and two PP readings to **Compare modifier attachments**. Add an appendix page for `one` and the refined NOM structure, plus partitives in an extension. Anchor pp. 142–156 and 165–170. | Learner can identify what `one` replaces and compare core NOM with the appendix’s refinement. The appendix diagram is labelled separately and uses the existing appendix convention where supported. |
| **8 · Embedded clauses** | Add a three-clause map with one row per clause, its span, host and function. Extend existing comparison pages with `if` versus `whether`, extraposition limits, a passive test for an object clause, and a temporal/causal `since` pair. Add a longer mixed analysis with feedback. Anchor pp. 171–187 and 191–195. | Learner can label each clause’s function and attach it to the correct head or phrase. Both readings of an ambiguous introducer are explained with context. |
| **9 · Wh-clauses** | Extend question examples to cover wh-determiner, possessive and degree patterns and another host for an embedded wh-question. Add a trace of a deeper gap. Add a restriction check for non-restrictive `that` and omission, and reuse Chapter 7’s `one` comparison. Put the competing C1 account in an extension. Anchor pp. 196–211 and 220–223. | Learner finds the exact gap through an embedding and can explain how attachment and introductory forms distinguish three clause types in NP. |
| **10 · Non-finite clauses** | Add a stacked auxiliary contrast and a non-finite relatives page with infinitival, -ing and passive participle examples. Expand **Ask what job the clause does** with extraposition, two A-complement patterns, and N-complement versus NP-modifier examples. Expand **Compare Type I and Type II** with subject and object control and the book’s borderline cases. Anchor pp. 224–244. | Learner tracks form, attachment, and controller separately, and explains which two gaps are present in a worked relative or passive example. Scope uncertain verb classifications to the book’s examples. |
| **11 · Grammar** | Connect recursion to Chapter 8’s embedded clauses, compare flat and binary coordination, then add an original mini grammar exercise that derives a new sentence and explains why a second sequence falls outside its rules. Add a short note on language varieties and the book’s discussion of describing an infinite language. Anchor pp. 254–268. | Learner can explain why more examples on a list never exhaust a repeatable rule, derive a novel structure, and distinguish the language described from the sentences a learner has heard. |

## Integration and verification

1. **Work one chapter at a time.** Begin with Chapters 1–3 to complete the tests, functions and labels reused later. Tag each coverage entry as taught and practised, taught only, or pending. A topic can close when its teaching example and practice are both in place, including in extensions. Then complete 4–6, 7–8, 9–10, and the Chapter 11 synthesis. For each new topic, record the printed page or figure, the article section that teaches it, the original worked example, and the practice check.
2. **Keep core reading manageable.** Use the existing ten-minute target as a target for the core route. Put extended cases in the chapter sidebar after their prerequisite page. Include at least one model explanation for each extension and measure reading time separately when learner trials resume.
3. **Keep completion and scoring compatible.** Start with unscored practice and stable existing section IDs. Optional extension visits should leave current completion and XP intact. If adding required visits or changing scored questions, issue a new content version with its matching answer key and section seed. Preserve existing results and test the transition before activating any version.
4. **Verify each lesson as it lands.** Run the catalog and chapter browser checks. Test desktop and mobile, read-ahead, resume, tree outline, every new activity, and feedback for both a correct answer and a likely mistake. Compare each revised answer against the actual example and cited edition. After the last chapter, run the combined preview, static build and existing assessment/progress suites.
5. **Record remaining review separately.** Keep unverified page anchors distinct from confirmed ones. Before any account scoring activation, use the existing [release and rollback rehearsal](../reports/syntax-foundations-revision-phase-5.md). Cambridge content and its account rules remain outside the Foundations rollout.
