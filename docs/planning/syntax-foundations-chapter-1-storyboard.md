## Outcomes and timing

1. Use omission, substitution or a question to support a proposed group in a specific sentence (pp. 10–15).
2. Read nested brackets and phrase markers. Trace a node to all and only the proposed words and distinguish direct from indirect coverage (pp. 13–17).
3. Explain how a change in attachment changes the interpretation of the same words (pp. 15–18).

Untested estimate **9 min 45 sec**. Introduction 40 sec, opening check 75 sec, concepts 100 sec, tree comparison and guided repair 140 sec, ambiguity transfer 100 sec, recap 35 sec, closing check 95 sec. Movement and the sense test are named briefly as supporting evidence. A full movement exercise, agreement/category tests, and extended book exercises are deferred. Time a first-time learner before treating the estimate as verified. If the tree repair takes the session past ten minutes, compare a single guided branch repair with allowing a longer first chapter.

## Page sequence and answer review

| Page | Learner decision and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | In “Mina paused [near [the stone arch]]”, identify the whole location and the landmark inside it. Define constituent, phrase and hierarchy. Offer five diagnostic items with a score-only result. |
| Test a group in context | Compare “Mina paused”, “Mina paused there”, and “Mina paused near it”. Use the intact sentence and question answers to distinguish the location from the landmark. Contrast “near the arch” with “near the arch that spans the canal” on the indicated reading. The landmark boundary expands when the descriptive clause is part of the landmark group. |
| Read and repair a phrase marker | Compare flat and nested versions of “near the stone arch”. Define the node and what it dominates, including the immediate branch from PHRASE-a to “near” and to PHRASE-b. A triangle can stand for the unexpanded inner phrase. Repair the flat tree for “beside the red gate” with the existing guided Builder. Accept any exercise structure with the intended direct outer branches and an inner phrase covering the three gate words in order. |
| Explain two readings | In “Nina spotted the climber with binoculars”, first describe the two readings, then trace each attachment. The binoculars phrase joins either the spotting group or the climber group. Transfer to “Ivo sketched the guide with a pencil”, with a hint and self-check. Accept paraphrases that correctly identify the sketching instrument and the guide holding the pencil. |
| Recap | Start with evidence, draw the boundary, trace the exact span, and check the reading. Explain why adjacency alone does not establish a constituent. Place the book references here. |
| Closing check | Five new items on diagnostic evidence, brackets, immediate dominance and ambiguity. Use Phase 2 submission, review and retakes. |
