# Chapter 5 proposal · Adverbials and other matters

**Status** · Implemented as a [local pilot](../reports/syntax-foundations-chapter-5-pilot.md). Gate 4.5 review and first-time learner timing are pending.

## Source and scope

Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. Read printed pp. 87–110 (PDF pp. 102–125) in the reference copy used for the earlier pilots. Visually checked figures [12], [15–16], [32], [35], [39], [46], [55a–b] and [58]. The current SynTutor lesson covers the time adjunct, two VP levels and one “did so” example. Keep that sequence and extend it to the book’s remaining distinctions.

| Book section | Teaching decision |
| --- | --- |
| Adjunct adverbials, pp. 87–88, [1–10] | Define adverbial as a function. A PP, AdvP or certain NPs can supply an adjunct. Use one short example of each. |
| Levels of Verb Phrase, pp. 88–92, [11–25] | Trace V and its complements in VP₁. Attach the adjunct beside VP₁ under VP₂. Compare identical-looking PPs with different jobs. Use “did so” with a changed time phrase to check the span. Mention that another adjunct can add another VP layer. Defer the full multiple-adjunct derivation. |
| Mobility of adverbials, pp. 92–93, [26–32] | Compare an adjunct before and after the VP it modifies. Show that position alone cannot determine scope. Acknowledge the book’s limit on drawing displaced and interposed VP adjuncts. |
| Phrasal verbs, pp. 93–95, [33–40] | Compare a movable particle with a preposition heading a PP. Use the pronoun placement test in a clear transitive example. Defer the full phrasal-verb inventory and ambiguous readings. |
| Ellipsis, pp. 96–98, [41–50] | In an explicit recipient context, contrast an understood complement with an optional event detail. Read E as the representation of the understood NP. Defer analyses where the verb may have a different lexical sense. |
| Sentence adverbials, pp. 98–101, [51–59] | Compare event manner with a comment on the whole statement. Define disjunct as a speaker’s comment and conjunct as a link to the surrounding discourse. Draw clear edge-position examples. Defer medial sentence-adverbial trees. |
| Discussion and exercises, pp. 101–110 | Reuse the structural comparisons in short original checks. Defer the extended ambiguity and verb-sense exercises. |

## Outcomes

1. Distinguish a V complement from a VP adjunct and justify the attachment with the phrase’s role and appropriate evidence.
2. Distinguish a particle from a preposition and an understood complement from an optional adjunct.
3. Distinguish VP adverbials from S adverbials by what they modify and where they attach.

## V5 diagram and feedback plan

| Example | Expected analysis and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| Nora inspected the bridge in June. | VP₁ contains V [trans] and object NP “the bridge”. VP₂ has VP₁ and PP “in June” as sisters ([12]). The editor repair restores this same relation. If the learner attaches the PP under VP₁, direct them to the parent of the whole verb–object group. |
| Mina repaired the tools in the shed. / Mina put the tools in the shed. | With “repaired”, the location is a sister of VP₁ in VP₂. With “put”, V [complex], the object NP and its locative object predicative PP are all immediate children of one basic VP ([15–16]). |
| Mara quietly checked the latch. / Mara checked the latch quietly. | The AdvP is a preceding or following sister of the smaller VP. Keep word order in both trees. Reserve this template for the clear positions illustrated by [31–32]. |
| Lea turned it off. / Lea stepped off the bus. | In the separated phrasal form, V [trans] [phrasal], NP and Prt are sisters under VP ([39–40]). “Off the bus” forms one PP with “off” before its NP. The assessment tests the particle distinction without requiring a verb feature for “stepped”. |
| Mina gave a map. | In the stated context, V [ditrans], NP → E for the understood recipient, and the overt NP “a map” are sisters ([46]). E is a notation mark for the understood element and contributes no spoken word. |
| Lea answered the question honestly. / Honestly, Lea answered the question. | “Honestly” is AdvP in both. With the event-manner reading it is a sister of VP within VP. With the speaker-comment reading it is a preceding sister of S within a higher S ([55a–b], p. 100). The interpretation provides the evidence. A comma is a clue, and the contrast does not extend to every fronted adverbial. |

## Draft checks

| Item | Prompt | Answer and feedback focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening 1 | In “The nurse checked the chart at noon”, which phrase tells when the check happened? | **at noon.** This group locates the event in time. |
| Opening 2 | In “Ari put the key on the hook”, which phrase tells where the key ended up? | **on the hook.** It gives the key’s resulting location. |
| Opening 3 | Which version keeps the meaning of “Sal switched off the light” when “the light” becomes “it”? | **Sal switched it off.** The particle follows the object pronoun here. |
| Opening 4 | Ren is collecting the class handouts. Someone says “Mina gave two copies”. Who is understood to receive them? | **Ren.** The context supplies the recipient. |
| Opening 5 | In “Luckily, the glass stayed intact”, what does “luckily” express? | **The speaker’s evaluation of what happened.** It comments on the whole statement. |
| Closing 1 | In “Lea scrubbed the pan after dinner, and Nia did so before lunch”, what does “did so” replace in this comparison? | **scrubbed the pan.** The time changes while the activity stays the same. |
| Closing 2 | In “Nia put the crate under the stairs”, how does the PP attach? | **Beside V and the object NP inside the basic VP.** The PP completes the placement pattern and locates the object. |
| Closing 3 | In “Ari folded it up”, what is “up” doing? | **It is the particle of “fold up”.** The pronoun intervenes between the verb and particle. |
| Closing 4 | Jo is the recipient in the conversation. In “Ari gave a ticket”, what does E represent in this analysis? | **The understood recipient NP.** The ticket remains the overt direct object. |
| Closing 5 | In “Unfortunately, the lamp broke”, what does “unfortunately” attach beside? | **The S “the lamp broke”, under a higher S.** The AdvP evaluates the whole event. |
