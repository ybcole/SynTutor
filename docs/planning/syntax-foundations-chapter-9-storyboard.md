## Source and scope

Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. Reviewed printed pp. 196–223 (PDF pp. 211–238). Visually checked [11b], [13], [26], [38–39], and [59–60]. The current lesson teaches an object wh-question with two gaps. Keep its inspection example and Builder repair, then extend the same reasoning to embedded interrogatives and relatives.

| Relative clauses, pp. 204–206, [27–43] | Compare an object relative with a noun-complement clause. The relative has a gap and joins a NOM. The noun complement gives the noun’s content and joins N. Defer the extended pro-NOM “one” test. |

## Outcomes and learner flow

1. Use a possible answer to identify a wh-expression’s grammatical role and locate its gap.
2. Distinguish C2 and C1 in a main question and an embedded wh-interrogative.
3. Distinguish a noun complement, a restrictive relative and a non-restrictive relative by their meaning and attachment.

## V9 diagram and feedback plan

- Main object question [11b]. Use S″ → C2 + S′ and S′ → C1 + S. C2 holds “what” with an NP gap beside the lexical V. C1 holds “is” with a separate gap under PROG [pres]. Keep the canonical worked tree and use the existing Builder checker for the detached NP. The caption identifies the lower VP parent and the difference between the two gaps.
- Stranded and fronted prepositions [13b]. In “Who is Nora inspecting the bridge with?”, the gap is an NP inside the PP headed by “with”. In “With whom is Nora inspecting the bridge?”, the whole PP is fronted and the gap occupies the PP’s former adjunct position. Use the possible answer with Ivo to establish the companion reading.
- Subject question [14] and Discussion 2. “Who is inspecting the bridge?” questions the subject. Use the Chapter 9 convention for an example with overt “is”. Scope any generalization about auxiliary fronting to the analysed examples. A bare lexical subject question such as “Who inspected the bridge?” needs no neutral do-support.
- Embedded wh-interrogative [26]. Under the embedded S″, C2 holds “what” and C1 has no word. The lexical object gap is inside S. “Was” remains under PROG [past] after Nora. Verify that C1 has neither an overt word nor an auxiliary movement gap.
- Relatives and noun complements [38–39], [44–54]. In the noun complement, “that” occupies C1 under S′ and the inner S is complete. In the relative variants, S″ joins a smaller NOM and the object gap is inside S. “Which” and relative “that” occupy C2 in this analysis. If the object relative omits the introductory form, keep its C2 position unfilled and preserve the object gap. Use the same trees to explain the role contrast before showing the optional variant.
- Relative scope [59–60]. In the restrictive example, the relative joins NOM within a larger NOM and is within the determiner’s scope. In the non-restrictive example, the relative joins the full NP “the lanterns” under a larger NP. Both clause fragments retain the same internal analysis, including the subject gap.

## Draft checks

| Item | Prompt | Answer and feedback focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening 1 | In a possible answer to “What is Eli packing?”, where would “the red bag” go? | **After “packing”.** It fills the direct-object position. |
| Opening 2 | In “Who is fixing the latch?”, which role does “who” ask about? | **Subject.** A possible answer names the person doing the fixing. |
| Opening 3 | In “Nia asked what Ren was carrying”, which group follows “what”? | **Ren was carrying.** The subject comes before “was” inside the embedded question. |
| Opening 4 | In “the claim that the bridge was unsafe”, what does the clause give? | **The content of the claim.** It tells what was claimed. |
| Opening 5 | In “The baskets which were damaged were returned”, which baskets does the statement say were returned? | **The damaged baskets.** The relative identifies the subset being discussed. |
| Closing 1 | In “Who did Ari speak to?”, where is the gap corresponding to “who”? | **The NP position after “to”.** The preposition remains inside S. |
| Closing 2 | In “Ren wondered what Ivo was reading”, how are the front positions filled? | **C2 contains “what” and C1 is unfilled.** “Was” remains inside S. |
| Closing 3 | Compare “the report that the roof leaked” and “the report that Ivo filed”. Which inner clause contains an object gap? | **Ivo filed.** The gap corresponds to the report. The first clause gives its content and is complete. |
| Closing 4 | In “the lamps which had faded”, what does the restrictive S″ join? | **The smaller NOM “lamps”.** The relative narrows the nominal under the determiner. |
| Closing 5 | In “The lamps, which had faded, were replaced”, what does the relative join? | **The whole NP “the lamps”.** The clause adds a separate comment about the already identified group. |
