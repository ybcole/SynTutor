# Chapter 3 proposal · Sentence structure: categories

**Status:** Implemented as a local pilot following the request to proceed. The first-time learner trial and Gate 4.3 review are pending.

## Source and scope

Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. Read printed pp. 46–66 (PDF pp. 61–81) in the copy recorded in the Chapter 1 source receipt. Visually checked the figures on printed pp. 51–53, 57 and 61–62. The existing SynTutor chapter focuses on adjective phrases and degree words. Keep that useful contrast and place it within the book’s broader sequence.

| Book section | Teaching decision |
| --- | --- |
| Nouns, pp. 47–49 | Introduce nouns by their grammatical behaviour, using plural forms and a noun within a group. Defer a full account of noun subclasses and exceptions. |
| Lexical and phrasal categories, pp. 50–53, [7], [14], [18] | Compare a noun with the NP it heads. A pronoun can occupy the position of the whole NP. A name or pronoun can form an NP on its own in this convention. |
| Adjectives and adverbs, pp. 54–55 | Use an adjective, a degree word and a general adverb. Teach the job of each in context, since word endings alone do not determine category. |
| Adjective Phrases and Adverb Phrases, pp. 55–56, diagrams (a–b) on p. 62 | Contrast `A` with `AP`, and `Adv` with `AdvP`. Trace `DEG` inside an AdvP that modifies an adjective. |
| Prepositions and Prepositional Phrases, pp. 56–57, [19a–b] | Return to the head–complement relation from Chapter 2. Show P with an NP complement, and the book’s analysis of locative “there” as a one-word PP. |
| Co-ordinate Phrases, pp. 57–62, [29–30], [40–41] | Compare two same-category conjuncts with the whole coordination. Use a pair of PPs as the primary case and defer mixed-category exceptions, lexical coordination trees, and degree scope in ambiguous coordination. |
| In-text diagrams, exercises and discussion, pp. 62–66 | Use a short original trace and transfer. Defer the longer category inventory and nested coordination exercises. |

## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish a word’s lexical category from the phrase category and identify the whole NP when it contains one word or several.
2. Use the head and its dependents to distinguish AP, AdvP and PP under the chapter’s V3 notation.
3. Identify the two constituents in a simple same-category coordination and name the category of the whole group.

## Expected V3 analyses

- `[NP [name Mina]]` and `[NP [pronoun they]]` follow [14] and [18]. For a multiword NP, highlight the whole group and its noun head. Keep any drawn internal labels within V3, with no later DET/NOM structure.
- `[AP [AdvP [DEG quite] [Adv clearly]] [A visible]]` follows in-text diagram (b). `[AP [DEG very] [A visible]]` follows (a). Accept a learner explanation that identifies “visible” as the adjective head and “clearly” as the head of the smaller AdvP.
- `[PP [P beside] [NP △ the bench]]` and `[PP [P there]]` follow [19a–b]. Teach the latter as the book’s analysis of this locative use.
- `[PP [PP beside the bench] and [PP near the heater]]` follows [41b], with the PP internals available on demand. The coordinator is shown as a terminal, and the parent and conjuncts share the PP category. Accept either quoted spans or a tree that preserves the full spans and the coordinator. Keep the exercise scoped to these clearly parallel PPs.

## Draft checks

| Item | Prompt | Reviewed target and feedback focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening 1 | Which words does “they” replace in “The glass jars fell”? | **The glass jars.** “Jars” alone leaves the modifiers and determiner behind. |
| Opening 2 | Which word supplies the main description in “very cold”? | **cold.** “Very” changes degree. |
| Opening 3 | Which word tells how someone spoke in “spoke rather softly”? | **softly.** “Rather” changes degree. |
| Opening 4 | Which whole group answers “Where did the puppy hide?” in “The puppy hid behind the couch”? | **behind the couch.** The couch supplies the landmark. |
| Opening 5 | Which two groups does “and” join in “The cats and the dogs slept”? | **the cats / the dogs.** The determiner belongs with each noun. |
| Closing 1 | In “Lea nodded”, what are the two levels for “Lea” in the Chapter 3 tree? | **A name inside a one-word NP.** The lexical and phrasal categories are distinct. |
| Closing 2 | In “The notice was unusually clear”, which category describes “unusually clear”? | **AP.** “Clear” is the adjective head and “unusually” modifies it. |
| Closing 3 | In “The guide answered very patiently”, what is the head of “very patiently” and what is the whole group’s category? | **patiently, AdvP.** “Very” marks degree. |
| Closing 4 | In the book’s analysis, what category is shared by the locative groups “under the desk” and “there” in “The bag is under the desk” and “The bag is there”? | **PP.** Both occupy the location position in this comparison. |
| Closing 5 | In “The hikers rested beside the stream and near the bridge”, what is coordinated? | **Two whole PPs forming one PP.** Both landmarks stay inside their own PPs. |
