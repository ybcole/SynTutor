# Syntax Foundations lesson structure

This map follows the reviewed [curriculum](../curriculum/README.md) and
[lesson rollout](syntax_foundations_lesson_rollout.md). Chapters 1–11 use
Burton-Roberts notation. The Cambridge companion explains a related distinction
in its own terminology.

## Page sequence

1. **Introduction.** Introduce the example in context and explain the bracketed groups. Use the regular lesson background for the example panel. After **What you’ll see in this chapter**, put **Key terms** in one transparent container. Give each term a plain definition that learners can use across examples. Identify notation specific to the tree conventions.
2. **Core concepts.** Build on the terms introduced on the first page. Use the same terms in the worked analysis, transfer practice, and recap.
3. **Worked analysis.** Let learners trace the structure in numbered steps. Put the visible explanation below the diagram in a neutral, bordered panel.
4. **Transfer.** Give the learner a new example and explain what makes the selected analysis work.
5. **Recap.** Summarize what the chapter demonstrated, why its distinction matters, and how the example shows it. Follow with a short procedure and a fresh check where appropriate.
6. **Closing check.** Keep the reviewed assessment, answer keys, progress rules, and XP.

The opening check displays the number correct out of three after completion.
It is diagnostic and has no passing threshold or XP effect. The existing
first response remains the synced completion marker. The full result is
recorded for new attempts on the current device, under the current learner
and lesson version. Older completions keep their completion status. After the closing check, its saved result also appears on the introduction page. When discussing examples, refer to their participants or the speaker in the third person and keep quoted sentences distinct from the explanation.

## Chapter map

| Chapter | Key terms | Worked lesson and transfer | What the recap should connect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Constituents | Constituent, hierarchy, replacement test, structural ambiguity | Trace the garden group in flat and nested trees, then compare two readings of the telescope example. | A node covers all and only its words. Different groupings can change meaning. |
| 2 · Subject and predicate | Subject, predicate, category, function | Split the robins sentence, test the boundary, then trace the NP and VP under S. | The two groups have jobs and category labels. An NP inside VP can have another job. |
| 3 · Adjective phrases | Head, adjective phrase, degree word | Compare *cheerful* and *very cheerful*, then identify the head and whole AP in a new description. | A stays the head when DEG is removed, and AP can cover one word or more. |
| 4 · Transitive verb phrases | Transitive verb, direct object, sister nodes | Trace *inspected* and *the bridge*, then mark the full object in another sentence. | The object NP joins V inside VP. The subject NP joins VP under S. |
| 5 · Adjuncts and VP layers | Complement, adjunct, inner and outer VP | Compare *inspected the bridge* with *inspected the bridge in June*, then find the inner VP in a fresh example. | The object joins V. The time PP joins the event-denoting inner VP. |
| 6 · Progressive auxiliaries | Progressive auxiliary, lexical VP, VP complement, tense | Compare the progressive with Chapter 5's time adjunct, then mark the lower VP and tense in a new sentence. | PROG heads the higher VP and carries tense. The verb and object remain together below. |
| 7 · Noun phrase structure | Determiner, demonstrative, nominal, head noun | Trace *those lanterns* from each word to NP, then apply the analysis to *these baskets*. | DET and NOM are immediate parts of NP. N and NOM can cover the same word at different levels. |
| 8 · That-clauses | Embedded clause, complementiser, S and S′ | Find the report inside the whole sentence, then select the starts of S′ and S in another report. | *That* sits in C beside S. S′ covers both. |
| 9 · Object wh-questions | Fronting, C1, C2, gap | Compare statement, yes/no question, and object question. Trace the two fronted words and their original positions. | The C1 auxiliary and C2 wh-expression correspond to different gaps. |
| 10 · To-infinitives | Infinitival *to*, non-finite, stem form, lexical VP | Compare present and past matrix verbs and distinguish the two uses of *to* in fresh examples. | *To* carries [-tense] in this analysis and takes a lexical VP. The larger verb can carry tense. |
| 11 · Grammar rules | Grammar, combination rule, recursion | Join two complete sentences and use the result in another application of the rule. | A reusable rule explains the grouping and why there is no fixed longest result in the toy grammar. |
| Cambridge companion | Written sentence, clause, embedded clause | Mark a sentence boundary and the embedded subject and predicate in a short passage. | A written sentence can contain more than one subject–predicate structure. |
