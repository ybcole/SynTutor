## Source map and scope

Noel Burton-Roberts, *Analysing Sentences*, third edition, Pearson, 2011, ISBN 9781408233740. Checked printed pp. 24–45 (PDF pages 39–60) against the same edition and page offset recorded in the Chapter 1 source receipt. The AI source pass checked the prose and diagrams [33–35], [42–43], [46] and [48]. The draft analyses and items below still need the chapter's post-implementation review and owner walkthrough.

| Book section | What the lesson will teach |
| --- | --- |
| Subject and predicate, pp. 24–28 | Start with the two largest groups in a simple declarative. Use the corresponding yes/no question to recover the whole subject, including words after its head noun. |
| Noun Phrase and Verb Phrase, pp. 29–31, [33–34] | Distinguish phrase category from grammatical function. Show the NP and VP as sisters under S. Contrast the subject NP with an NP inside the VP. |
| Dependency and function, pp. 31–38, [35–48] | Define sisters, head, modifier, and complement. Apply omission to each immediate part in the same reading. Show how the whole phrase can be optional even when a part is needed within it. |
| Summary and exercises, pp. 38–45 | Use a new sentence to connect the three relations and explain the evidence for each answer. |

Defer exceptional subjects, sentence-level adverbial attachment, full lexical categories, and the detailed verb-complement inventory. Keep “in these simple statements” as the scope of the top split. Describe omission as evidence in a specified construction and interpretation. A word that can appear alone in a different construction does not decide the relation in the example.

## Expected trees and answers

- **Long subject.** `[S [NP △ The lantern by the stairs] [VP △ is glowing]]`. The long NP and VP are sisters. Question formation is evidence for the declarative boundary. The lesson does not teach an inversion tree here.
- **Two NPs.** `[S [NP △ Mira] [VP checked [NP △ the lantern]]]`. The first NP is directly under S. The second is directly under VP. Keep the early Chapter 2 level of detail from [34]. Use V2 for serialized activities. Keep role names in captions or overlays.
- **Dependencies.** In “Tessa repaired a loose hinge”, the subject is “Tessa” and the predicate is “repaired a loose hinge”. Within the predicate, “a loose hinge” completes “repaired” in the intended reading. Within that NP, “loose” modifies “hinge”. For the last relation, show the relevant branches or use a separate dependency diagram patterned on [48]. Keep later lexical category labels out of the early V2 diagram. Accept equivalent explanations that identify the whole complement and explain which change leaves the intended reading intact.
- **Transfer.** In “Leo tightened a loose bolt”, the subject is “Leo”, the predicate is “tightened a loose bolt”, the complement is “a loose bolt”, and “loose” modifies “bolt”. Accept equivalent labels in prose and explain why just “bolt” would omit part of the complement in this sentence.

## Draft assessments

| Check | Prompt | Key and distractor rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening 1 | In “The boat near the pier is rocking”, which words identify what is rocking? | **The boat near the pier.** “The boat” stops early. “Near the pier” names only its location. |
| Opening 2 | In “The small bells rang twice”, which group says what happened? | **rang twice.** “rang” omits part of the predicate. “The small bells” identifies the subject. |
| Opening 3 | In “Inez found the kite”, which group stands in the subject position? | **Inez.** “the kite” occurs within the predicate. “Inez found” crosses the split. |
| Opening 4 | In “Rui carried a heavy parcel”, what can be left out to give “Rui carried a parcel”? | **heavy.** Removing “parcel” loses the nominal head. Removing “a” leaves an ill-formed singular NP here. |
| Opening 5 | In “The lid is rattling”, which yes/no question preserves the meaning? | **Is the lid rattling?** Moving the wrong string breaks the question. Substituting “was” changes the tense. |
| Closing 1 | In “The violin in its case was rattling”, where is the split? | **After “case”.** “Was the violin in its case rattling?” keeps the full NP together. Splitting after “violin” or “was” cuts a phrase. |
| Closing 2 | In “The visitor greeted the caretaker”, both people are named by NPs. Which is the subject, and why? | **“The visitor”, because its NP is directly under S.** The caretaker NP is inside VP. Being a person or an NP alone does not establish the function. |
| Closing 3 | In the V2 tree for “The shutters creaked”, which nodes are sisters? | **The NP for “the shutters” and the VP for “creaked”.** They share S as their immediate parent. S and NP have different roles in the tree. |
| Closing 4 | In “Nico folded a damp towel”, which word modifies “towel”? | **damp.** It adds a description and can be omitted. “Towel” is the head. “Folded” heads the larger predicate. |
| Closing 5 | In “Ava slipped beside the counter”, the whole location can be left out. Why does “the counter” still belong with “beside” in the original location phrase? | **It supplies the landmark required by this use of “beside”.** “Beside” alone leaves the intended relation incomplete. The optionality of the whole phrase does not make every part inside it optional. |

Opening items 1–3 and 5 draw on pp. 24–31. Opening item 4 and closing item 4 draw on pp. 32–35. Closing items 1–3 draw on pp. 26–32 and [33–35]. Closing item 5 draws on pp. 35–37 and [43–46]. The opening diagnostic presents the learner with questions before the teaching sequence. Feedback appears only for the closing check.
