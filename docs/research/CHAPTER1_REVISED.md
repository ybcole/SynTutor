## **1.1 PROJECT CONTEXT** {#1.1-project-context}

	Emerging technologies in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and their integration in language education has made a transformative impact in computer assisted language learning (CALL) into more advanced systems that can adapt to the needs of learners and explain complex theories more easily. Natural Language Processing (NLP) technologies have significantly advanced the capabilities of these applications, opening up new possibilities for machines to understand human language and its complexities (Ansari, 2025). Tools that identify grammatical errors, suggest lexical alternatives, and improve the structure of a paragraph have been widely used in different fields. However, although these models have achieved high accuracy in detecting surface-level syntactic errors and improving sentence structure, they largely operate in a "black box" where explanations are hidden from the user (Deng et al., 2025). Modern tools highlight a given text and provide another sequence, becoming correctors rather than teachers because they do not explain the underlying grammatical reasoning. There is a transparency ceiling as the lack of clarification  leaves learners guessing how proficient sentences are written, creating a dependency on programs that create their work instead of actually improving their independent linguistic competence.  
	To move beyond this "black box" limitation, tools must shift their focus from correcting sentences to a system that can analyze, provide feedback, and generate explanations that enable users to become more proficient. A clear and concise tool that can be utilized by AI to bridge this gap are syntax trees. Syntax trees are used to analyze a sentence's grammar by providing a hierarchical data structure. It shows a sentence's grammatical organization which helps learners to identify the relationships between words and phrases. By splitting a sentence into its different constituent elements such as noun phrases (NP), verb phrases (VP), and prepositional phrases (PP), the syntax tree can mathematically illustrate syntactic and dependency relations. The visualization of these rules can enhance learning of learners. Recent studies on English as a Foreign Language also demonstrate how using visual tools can allow learners to understand linguistics and grammatical relationships better than text-based instructions. (Bochari & Hastini, 2025).

	Despite the educational benefits of syntax trees, however, interpreting them manually is too technical and are often challenging for new learners to use alone. This is where Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) becomes an intervention. XAI covers methods to make machine learning systems more interpretable by explaining their decisions to users. With this application, users can not only view the visual structures of sentences, but also receive an explanation they can learn from. The XAI transforms the system into a learning agent, with interpretable, rule-based reasoning that ensures learners do not encounter a "black box", as they can understand what can be improved on their sentences (Selvi, 2024). The creation of an explainable and accessible phrase-structure syntax tree generator becomes an effective system to help both learners to analyze sentences and become more competent in linguistics. The researchers propose SynTutor, an integrated system that utilizes NLP constituency parsing combined with deterministic reasoning to support efficient identification of grammatical structures. By using XAI, SynTutor automates and explains syntax trees while being an accessible learning application. It is designed to provide learners with structural transparency that grammar tools lack, assisting them in gaining a more profound understanding of linguistics and improving their formal writing abilities.

## **1.2 PURPOSE AND DESCRIPTION** {#1.2-purpose-and-description}

The primary purpose of this study is to develop SynTutor, an integrated educational system that bridges abstract linguistic theory with modern computational methods. By combining AI-driven Natural Language Processing (NLP) with interactive phrase-structure syntax trees, the project transforms traditional grammar instruction into a visual and highly accessible format. The overarching goal is to provide a unified framework that supports both active learner comprehension and advanced linguistic analysis, effectively moving users beyond a passive reliance on surface-level automated corrections toward genuine grammatical competence.

The system creates instant grammar trees and clear explanations, helping teachers deliver engaging, data-driven lessons.

Functionally, SynTutor is designed as an intelligent tutoring agent that facilitates the structural analysis of English text. The system provides a flexible environment capable of accommodating extensive textual inputs, maintaining rigorous syntactic precision by isolating data at the sentential level for processing. Users navigate through their submitted text by selecting specific sentences, which the system then parses to generate a corresponding hierarchical phrase-structure syntax tree. Each tree annotates components with their specific grammatical roles, such as parts of speech, tense, agreement, and modification. Crucially, SynTutor integrates Explainable AI (XAI) to supplement these visual diagrams with clear, natural-language explanations. Rather than simply highlighting errors, the system explicitly details the underlying rules governing each syntactic relationship. This dual approach of sentence-level visualization and rule-based XAI feedback enables users to deeply understand grammatical structures and provides educators and researchers with a practical, real-time tool for demonstrating linguistic concepts.

SynTutor's technical architecture consists of four coordinated layers:

1. **Neural Constituency Parsing (benepar_en3)** — A pre-trained, non-generative neural constituency parser that resolves English syntactic ambiguity by ranking candidate phrase-structure parses and returning the single highest-scoring S-rooted tree for each sentence. The parser is applied at inference time, not trained, and identical serialized input always yields the same tree.

2. **Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix** — A hand-authored rule module that evaluates every parent-child edge in the generated syntax tree across 11 grammatical categories, classifying each edge into one of three states: valid, error, or unlisted. Valid edges map to positive role explanation templates; error edges return a specific C-* code, an error explanation, and a repair suggestion; unlisted edges are recorded for gold-corpus audits.

3. **Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer** — A licensing algorithm that demands positive evidence for every open-class word. For each noun, verb, adjective, or adverb leaf, the algorithm queries the deterministic lexicon (lemminflect and inflect) to ensure the surface form is a valid inflection for its parser-assigned POS tag. It strips suffixes to catch malformed inflections (e.g., "childs", "runned", "stupids") and checks relative-clause rules (2.7 and 5.4). Each checker runs in isolation so a single crashing checker degrades the verdict to unverified rather than crashing the system.

4. **Hybrid Acceptability/Correction-Search channel** — A trained CoLA-fine-tuned RoBERTa acceptability classifier (textattack/roberta-base-CoLA) that decides whether the sentence reads as acceptable English. A sentence is grammatical only if both the rule matrix and the model accept it. When the matrix is clean but the model rejects the sentence, a masked-language-model sweep (distilbert-base-uncased) performs a deterministic correction search, masking one word at a time to find a single-token replacement that shares the same lemma and flips the classifier to acceptable. The finding is promoted to ungrammatical (C-MLM-ANOMALY) only if the repaired tree passes the Constraint Matrix cleanly.

## **1.3 STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM** {#1.3-statement-of-the-problem}

Existing Grammar Error Correction (GEC) tools are limited in their ability to elucidate the underlying structural rules that govern correct grammatical construction and are only capable of detecting surface-level errors. As these tools correct without explanation, it leads to learners relying on tools instead of cultivating actual grammatical competence. Although syntax trees are a well-established representation for grammatical structure, existing parsers produce static, non-interactive diagrams devoid of contextual justification or instructional scaffolding, leaving learners with a visual representation of the proper and improper structure of grammar without giving them a deep understanding of the reason.

	Therefore, this study aims to develop SynTutor, an explainable, interactive intelligent tutoring system that supports not only grammatical correction but also explicit grammar learning through structured, visual feedback. Specifically, the study seeks to address the following questions:

1. How can an NLP-based parsing pipeline — comprising spaCy preprocessing, the benepar_en3 neural constituency parser, and a recursive-descent parse-string decoder — generate phrase-structure syntax trees for English texts, from single sentences to extended passages?  
2. How can each node of the phrase-structure syntax tree be annotated with its grammatical role and provide natural-language explanations using an Explainable AI component grounded in the Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix and Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer?  
3. How can an interface be designed to be interactive that allows learners to visualize sentence structures, experiment with valid sentence variants, and access explanations for each node?  
4. How can the system provide educators, researchers, and scholars with a tool for demonstrating grammatical concepts, interpreting parser outputs, and diagnosing recurring errors in learner corpora?  
5. Is SynTutor an effective learning tool for improving grammar comprehension, reducing learners' dependence on grammar error correction tools, and providing understandable explanations?

## **1.4 RESEARCH OBJECTIVES** {#1.4-research-objectives}

### **1.4.1 General Objective** {#1.4.1-general-objective}

	To develop an integrated and explainable AI-based (XAI) system that uses phrase-structure syntax trees to support grammar learning, enable real-time sentence analysis, and facilitate both educational and linguistic research, anchored by a Neural Constituency Parser (benepar_en3), a Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix, a Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer, and a Hybrid Acceptability/Correction-Search channel.

### **1.4.2 Specific Objectives** {#1.4.2-specific-objectives}

1. To implement NLP-based parsing capable of generating phrase-structure syntax trees from input text, from single sentences to extended passages, using spaCy (en_core_web_md) preprocessing and the benepar_en3 neural constituency parser with recursive-descent parse-string decoding.  
2. To annotate each node of the syntax tree with its grammatical role and provide natural-language explanations using an Explainable AI component grounded in the Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix and Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer, making the parser's reasoning understandable to learners.  
3. To design an interactive interface that allows learners to visualize sentence structures, experiment with valid sentence variants, and access explanations for each node.  
4. To provide educators, researchers, and scholars with a tool for demonstrating grammatical concepts, interpreting parser outputs, and diagnosing recurring errors in learner corpora.  
5. To evaluate the system's effectiveness in improving grammar comprehension, learner independence, and the clarity of the explanations by conducting a system test and questionnaire with BSCS learners and English/Linguistics instructors, using Paired Samples t-Test, System Usability Scale (SUS), and Expert Validation.

## **1.5 HYPOTHESIS** {#1.5-hypothesis}

	The study hypothesizes that integrating NLP parsing with an XAI educational layer can effectively diagnose, visualize, and explain structural grammar in real-time. The hypothesis is as follows:  
**Null Hypothesis (H01):** The integrated neural constituency parser (benepar_en3) can generate phrase-structure syntax trees with no statistically significant degree of accuracy across varying levels of English complexity.

**Alternative Hypothesis(Ha1):** The integrated neural constituency parser (benepar_en3) generates phrase-structure syntax trees with a high degree of accuracy across varying English sentence complexity.  
**Null Hypothesis (H02):** Grounding the Explainable AI (XAI) diagnostic engine on the Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix and Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer does not significantly reduce grammatically unsound feedback, resulting in generative hallucinations or unrated edge errors.

**Alternative Hypothesis (Ha2):** Grounding the XAI on the Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix and Positive-Evidence Lexical Licensing layer reduces grammatically unsound feedback, resulting in linguistically verifiable and context-aware explanations.

**Null Hypothesis (H03)**: There is no statistically significant difference in student scores measuring deep-structure grammatical competence before and after using SynTutor

**Alternative Hypothesis (Ha3):** The visualization of phrase-structure syntax trees, combined with the XAI feedback grounded in deterministic reasoning, improves a learner's comprehension of deep-structure grammatical rules compared to traditional grammar-checking software.

**Null Hypothesis (H04):** SynTutor does not meet acceptable usability standards for student learners and linguistic educators as measured by the System Usability Scale.

**Alternative Hypothesis (Ha4):** SynTutor is highly accessible, usable, and reliable, according to the System Usability Scale, as an education tool for both learners and linguistic educators.

## **1.6 SIGNIFICANCE OF THE STUDY** {#1.6-significance-of-the-study}

The development of SynTutor presents a significant contribution to the fields of language education, computational linguistics, and artificial intelligence by integrating Natural Language Processing (NLP) with Explainable AI (XAI) to generate interactive phrase-structure syntax trees. This study provides distinct, practical benefits to the following specific groups:

**For English Learners:** SynTutor acts as a powerful, interactive visual scaffold that transforms passive grammar correction into an active learning experience. Instead of merely accepting generic automated edits, learners are provided with an interactive phrase-structure syntax tree that visually maps exactly how individual words group into phrases and clauses. This visualization serves as an "advance organizer," allowing learners to comprehend the fundamental hierarchical mechanics of language. Furthermore, unlike traditional "black-box" grammar checkers that flag errors without context, SynTutor provides understandable, rule-based reasoning for every syntactical relationship. This contextual feedback empowers students to internalize grammatical rules rather than guess at them, providing immediate, guided practice that reduces their long-term reliance on automated editing tools and ultimately develops them into highly independent and competent writers.

**For English Educators and Teachers:** The system serves as a novel and highly practical instructional resource that can be integrated directly into classroom environments to demonstrate complex grammatical concepts in real time. Teaching deep-structure grammar is often challenging due to its abstract nature, but SynTutor provides educators with the visual tools necessary to systematically prove to students exactly why a particular sentence structure succeeds or fails. By generating immediate, mathematically sound phrase-structure syntax trees paired with pedagogical XAI explanations derived from the Deterministic Linguistic Constraint Matrix, the system bridges the persistent gap between theoretical linguistic instruction and practical, engaging application, allowing teachers to deliver more dynamic and data-driven language lessons.

**For Linguists and Language Researchers:** SynTutor offers a sophisticated framework that concretely operationalizes abstract linguistic theory—specifically probabilistic phrase-structure rules—by linking it with real-time NLP capabilities. For researchers, the tool eliminates the tedious process of manual diagramming, allowing them to easily explore sentence variants and test complex grammatical hypotheses instantaneously. Additionally, the system provides a structured methodology for corpus analysis and diagnostics; researchers can utilize the deterministic XAI output to efficiently identify, categorize, and diagnose recurring structural errors within large datasets of learner corpora, thereby advancing empirical research in language acquisition and syntactic priming.

**For the Field of Educational Technology and AI Developers:** From a technical and developmental perspective, this study demonstrates a highly effective integration of NLP parsing and Explainable AI within an educational setting. While contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs) and neural text generation systems achieve high accuracy, they typically operate opaquely, leaving users blind to the underlying logic of their outputs. SynTutor directly addresses the industry's call for transparent and accountable technology by anchoring its AI within a deterministic framework. By explicitly annotating each tree node with its syntactic role and explaining the AI's reasoning in natural language derived from a verifiable constraint matrix, this study provides a working blueprint for solving the "black box" problem in modern educational software.

## **1.7 SCOPE AND LIMITATION** {#1.7-scope-and-limitation}

	This study focuses on the development and evaluation of SynTutor, a system based on Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) intended to help with grammar learning by generating and analyzing phrase-structure syntax trees. The system utilizes Natural Language Processing (NLP) to handle English text, in the form of sentences. It provides grammar feedback based on syntactic structures, such as parts of speech, tense, agreement, and modifiers. It also includes an explainable AI feature that gives clear, easy-to-understand explanations for the flagged node in the syntax tree. Additionally, there is an interactive interface that lets users visualize and explore sentence structures in real time. The system is assessed for its ability to improve grammar understanding, usability, learner independence, and the clarity of explanations among Computer Science students, English students, and English professors from De La Salle University \- Dasmariñas.  
	However, the study has several limitations. The system is limited to the English language and does not support multilingual input. It utilizes constituency-based phrase \-structure trees as its primary structural representation and does not expose dependency-based analyses to the learner; dependency metadata produced by the POS tagger is used only internally for preprocessing and is not surfaced in the interface. The analysis focuses on syntax and does not address semantic meaning, pragmatic context, or writing style. Additionally, the evaluation takes place with a small group of participants from students and professors at De La Salle University \- Dasmariñas. Their technical background may not fully represent the wider target user population. The evaluation also occurs within the timeframe of the thesis, so it does not consider long-term assessment of learner independence, grammar retention, or sustained learning outcomes. This limitation may impact the generalizability of the findings. Finally, the study does not include a control group; therefore, causal claims about SynTutor's superiority over traditional grammar-checking software cannot be made from the present design.
