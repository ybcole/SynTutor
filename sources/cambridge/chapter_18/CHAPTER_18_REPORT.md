## Source-grounded safeguards
- Lexical bases are **not** invariably independent attested singular or other inflectional forms: plural-only defective lexemes demonstrate the distinction.
- The complex internal morphology of a lexical base does not by itself change how the base is inflected; internal word-formation is reserved for Chapter 19.
- Most inflectional forms are formed directly from the base, but **some** depend on an intermediate inflected form. The record must not generalise this to all past participles.
- Neither an orthographic change nor a suffix alternant is necessarily irregular: regularity depends on whether the rule is lexeme-independent.
- Consonant changes in the plurals of forms such as thief are lexeme-specific even when plural suffixation is general; no blanket f-to-v rule is licensed.
- Syncretism requires identity of form **and distinction of grammatical paradigm cells**; it is not the same concept as lexical base.
- This subsection does not give the full conditioning environments for -s/-es alternation or an exhaustive list of minor morphological processes. Those remain deferred.
- The eight new records are classifications and tendencies, **not grammaticality constraints**.
