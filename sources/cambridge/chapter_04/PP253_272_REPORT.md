## Important exception/counterexample notes

- p.254: object passive test is only one-way; nominative pronoun PC is formal, and number matching is not syntactic agreement.
- pp.255–257: semantically PC-like NPs with represent, constitute, provide and make can still be syntactic objects; note the exceptional subject-oriented strike.
- pp.258–260: spatial and property goals can be marked or unmarked; into-state goal NPs retain locative syntax; possession roles do not equal grammatical S/O.
- pp.261–262: optional depictives are adjuncts, optional resultatives require lexical licensing; depictive orientation can be toward S or O.
- pp.263–265: sense verb and verb-of-becoming inventories have adjective/nominal/infinitival limitations and regional/register qualifications.
- pp.266–268: specifying versus ascriptive is a distinction of variable/value versus theme/property; reversal cannot be treated as a blanket correction.
- pp.269–271: specifying values depend on clause type, reflexive/reciprocal connectedness, aspect, NPI scope; definite PCs and indefinite variables exist.
- p.272: limited specifying remain/become constructions; idioms and verb-preposition sequences need not be syntactic verb constituents.
