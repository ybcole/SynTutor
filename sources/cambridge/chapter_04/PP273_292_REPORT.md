## Source layout, exceptions and false-positive checks

- pp.273–274: lexical idioms need not be syntactic constituents; a selected PP remains a PP and idioms can select clauses.
- pp.275–277: fixed and mobile selected prepositions differ under fronting, coordination, inserted adjuncts and passives; no verb-preposition rebracketing follows from mere fossilisation.
- p.276: passives can fail with a selected PP yet succeed with some unspecified locative PPs; passive availability is not simply selectedness.
- pp.278–280: six frames distinguish the object's governing verb/preposition and whether as takes a predicative; supply versus give alternation is structural.
- p.280: downstairs can precede a very heavy postposed NP though not an ordinary simple object; particle definition is weight-sensitive.
- p.281: the exact qualification unstressed matters in object pronoun placement; transitive out without of is AmE regional variation.
- pp.282–283: PP-fronting is sufficient but not necessary for transitive PP status, given fixed lexical prepositions; read through and look over have PP/intransitive-PP alternants.
- pp.284–286: locative referents can be implicit, and idiom lexical senses are highly polysemous; fossilisation varies by sense.
- p.287: particle placement around two objects has weak-pronoun and phrase-length restrictions; look forward to consists of two syntactic PPs.
- pp.288–289: verb NP transitive-PP idioms fall into four passive classes; noun modification tends to inhibit some passives but is not banned.
- pp.290–292: verb+adjective/verb+verb idioms vary in syntax; light verbs may add quantification, change aspectual interpretation and admit alternative PP attachment.
