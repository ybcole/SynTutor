## Caveats and exceptions checked

- pp.308–309: semantic middle is syntactically active; generalisation/manner restriction has lexical exceptions, and ditransitive lexical frames distinguish five classes rather than all transference being double-object.
- p.309: explain/donate are to-only in this variety, unlike tell/give, with possible dialectal and historical variation; classes are determined per verb sense.
- p.310: PP and double-object choices depend on discourse/weight; recipient versus spatial goal distinction excludes false double-object analyses for movement.
- p.311: goods-beneficiary vs services-beneficiary favors distinct frames but personal pronouns and directive speech can license regional exceptions; fine/cost/charge Oi may denote source, not recipient.
- p.312: forgive-for and serve-with have an Od in the monotransitive clause despite semantically resembling earlier Oi; issue British forms are restricted; light give Oi omission may be blocked.
- p.313: omission of recipient sometimes leaves an understood participant while omission of beneficiary may eliminate it; fine versus charge amount omissions are lexically restricted.
- p.314: present and blame participant alignment/omission differ; spray/load selected object often determines which endpoint counts as finished.
- p.315: put theme-only, fill locative-only; drain versus remove/deprive alignments are lexical, some accepted by some speakers only.
- p.316: the engraved inscription is factitive; search locative versus quest for-PP, some verbs allow both; instrument/theme object with hit requires a location.
- p.317: hit alternants need not share truth conditions with throw/break; build/produce/change source-goal alternants are verb-specific and not grammatical transformations by default.
- p.318: swarm and related locative-subject effects have a holistic pragmatic association that may disappear under altered locations/prepositions.
- p.319: intransitive develop goal/source alignment and trivalent wash/wipe reduction do not imply the same role when the theme is unexpressed.
- p.320: a noun denoting an instrument can be bivalent subject only with selected verbs; *A spoon ate it* marks a semantic anomaly (#) rather than identical syntactic ill-formedness; price/temporal subject alternatives are limited.
- p.321: genitive/of internal dependents can combine formerly separate complements, but break/discern lack corresponding trivalent constructions.
