## Tables, diagrams, footnotes, exceptions

- p.233 factitive theme and beneficiary are semantic classifications, not direct-object or subject tests.
- pp.234–235 canonical agent/patient alignment is a prototype, not a universal definition of subject.
- p.236 finite subject requirement has licensed exceptions for imperative, nonfinite and conversational reductions.
- pp.237–239 case, agreement, wh order, tags and reduction jointly support subject identification; coordinated NP can be one subject.
- pp.239–241 the tabulated S properties distinguish passivisation from object fronting; the latter does not change S/O functions; nonapplicable table entries retained.
- pp.241–243 dummy it and there pass subject tests; displaced/extraposed material remains non-subject; informal there's plus plural is variety-sensitive.
- p.244 subject–auxiliary inversion must not be conflated with final subject postposing.
- pp.245–247 passive is sufficient but not necessary evidence of objecthood; object position has particle/heavy-NP/preposing exceptions.
- p.248 only the double-NP construction is analysed as ditransitive; to/for PP is not Oi; some regional orders with two pronouns vary.
- pp.249–250 double-object second passives and Oi/fronting/depicitive contrasts have gradient and lexical restrictions, so no blanket error detector.
- pp.251–252 single-object canonical clauses only have Od; nominal predicatives may be nonreferential and distinct from Od.
