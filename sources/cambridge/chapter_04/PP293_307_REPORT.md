## Exception and paradigm audit

- pp.293–295: light give and make may retain ditransitive properties of the verb, but noun complement patterns differ; gift/advice/kiss/description and donation frame restrictions must not be homogenized.
- p.295: have/take are dialect preferences in overlapping lexical sets, while have is also stative in some senses.
- pp.296–297: role-alignment and valency matrices define four subtypes in each alternation family; voice and finite/nonfinite clause contrasts are intentionally separate.
- pp.298–299: conative at alternants often change entailments; directional transitives may imply endpoint whereas PP alternants do not always do so.
- p.300: theme/source role alignment switches between emission transitives and their from-PP intransitives.
- p.301: imperative instructional object omission is register-wide, but omitted definite objects depend on lexeme; enjoy is not automatically intransitive.
- pp.302–303: reflexive and reciprocal omissions exclude clothe/resemble and other superficially similar lexemes; transitive kiss does not inherently entail reciprocity.
- pp.303–304: bodily part, generic human, alcohol-specific and ordinary indefinite categories are not one single null-object rule; negative/dynamic conditions matter.
- pp.304–305: read versus peruse or eat versus devour; intransitives often describe activities while object expressions may describe accomplishments; cognate/reaction objects have lexical and passive limits.
- pp.306–307: construct/destroy do not freely alternate with inchoatives, and several alternations are restricted by word sense or argument type; English middle is semantically descriptive, active in form.
