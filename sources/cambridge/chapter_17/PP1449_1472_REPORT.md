## Linguistic safety and deferred independent QA

- **pp.1451–1455:** Context-relative deixis and discourse-supported anaphora cannot be judged grammatical in isolation; indexing acts, shifted centres, and speaker changes matter.
- **pp.1454–1456:** A pronoun with multiple candidates is not automatically ungrammatical. Anticipatory anaphora differs from retrospective anaphora and requires later syntactic tests.
- **pp.1457–1460:** Bound-variable, clause-antecedent, split-antecedent and missing-antecedent relations should not be reduced to strict NP coreference; only the stated local reflexive mismatch is spec-only diagnostic.
- **pp.1461–1463:** Discourse deixis, pro-NP/nominal/clause labels and pro-PP forms cannot be collapsed into one traditional pronoun category; B-R confirmation is pending.
- **pp.1464–1468:** Third-person reference can designate the speaker or addressee; inclusivity, generic you, plural you, authorial we and role-play carry distinct grammatical and pragmatic conditions.
- **pp.1469–1472:** Gender/number information need only be referentially compatible. Non-anaphoric, quasi-anaphoric, institutional and generic third-person uses are genuine English patterns, not universal error flags. The separate Chapter 5 treatment is needed before detector licensing.
