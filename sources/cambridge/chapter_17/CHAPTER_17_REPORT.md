## Source-grounded interpretation safeguards
- A proper name's varying referent does not automatically make the name deictic; a deictic meaning is systematically related to features of the utterance-act (p. 1451).
- Deictic expression and deictic marker are distinct where the marker is only part of an NP or time expression (p. 1452).
- Pointing, eye contact and printed diagrammatic devices can support deixis, but a gesture is not always necessary. Context and referent visibility matter (pp. 1452–1453).
- Speaker-centred interpretation is the default, with shifts across conversational speakers and occasional shifts to reader time or a reported person's perspective (p. 1453).
- The licensing conditions of reported temporal-centre shifts are deferred to Chapter 17 §10.2. These are semantic/discourse rules, not grammar-error constraints.
