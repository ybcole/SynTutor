## Critical contrasts retained

- Headlines and communication-reporting presents can refer to recent past events. The evidential reporting verb typically backgrounds the earlier communicative act.
- The main-clause present futurate is constrained by what is already settled or scheduled; mere subjective confidence in an unpredictable future outcome is not enough. The modal *will* allows a less constrained prediction. A present progressive also supports arranged futurates.
- Future-time present in many temporal and conditional subordinate clauses is **not** subject to the same main-clause scheduling condition. Relative, interrogative, comparative, hope/bet and covert mandative environments have their own licensing.
- A future-present subordinate interpretation can relate to the speech time or to an orientation time in a future reporting/thinking event; these meanings must be kept distinct.
- Past tense can refer to a whole past occurrence or to a past reference interval within a state. A polite preterite request commonly implicates a currently valid wish; this is not a separate inflection or a categorical error.
