## Exceptions, interpretation and anti-false-positive review

- **Perfect omission is conditional, not universal (p. 147).** In short temporal adjuncts, after/before/as soon as may communicate similar order with or without perfect. With longer situations, omission can change whether an event preceded an entire activity or occurred after its completion.
- **Anteriority has scope (pp. 147–148).** A repeated or distributive context may support narrow or wide readings. A perfect in the infinitival complement of would like may describe the earlier preference, the lower event, or a pleonastic second marking. These patterns must not be automatically removed as redundant.
- **Remote preterite is not equivalent to past time (pp. 148–150).** Present/future remote conditionals and wishes can use preterite. Open and remote conditionals differ in the likelihood/stance they convey; remote marking does not formally entail that the antecedent is false.
- **Remote conditional pairing has exceptions (p. 149).** A canonical remote protasis uses preterite/irrealis and the main clause a modal auxiliary in a preterite form where available. Modal auxiliaries lacking a distinct past inflection may remain unchanged; matrix imperatives and nonmodal matrices do not simply become remote by tensing both verbs.
- **Perfect has two possible modal roles (p. 150).** In a past-time remote protasis it expresses anteriority while the preterite expresses remoteness. In a rarer doubly remote present/future case it can add remoteness; a third recursive past layer is not a valid general strategy.
- **Formal and nonstandard forms (p. 151).** Singular irrealis were and preterite was vary in formality. Remote be to is distinct from open conditional uses of be to. Nonstandard had've and would have in remote protases are dialectally attested and must not be turned into claims about the capabilities of English speakers.
- **Backshift is an interpretation, not a rewrite (pp. 151–154).** Backshift is licensed by past matrix tense or by a past matrix situation, including a non-finite remembered telling. Must/ought may retain present form where no preterite exists. The backshifted preterite has a matrix-derived orientation that can itself be non-past; ordinary preterite locates time earlier than its orientation. In reported earlier-past content, perfect supplies the anterior relation. This phenomenon occurs outside indirect speech.
