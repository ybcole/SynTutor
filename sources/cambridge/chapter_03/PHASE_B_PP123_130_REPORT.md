## Semantic and exception safeguards

- Iterative punctual achievements may describe an overall activity; habitual series can be stative at the whole-situation level even if each occurrence is dynamic. The same verb cannot be assigned a single invariant situation type independent of arguments and context.
- Perfective versus imperfective are viewpoint meanings, not English inflectional tenses. The English perfect is a separate category from perfective aspectuality. Ordinary simple tense may receive imperfective interpretation under habitual or stative readings.
- Temporal variables Tr (reference), To (orientation), Tsit (situation), and Td (deictic time) are distinct; past morphosyntax can refer to a contextual non-deictic time rather than a time before utterance.
- A dynamic simple present can legitimately be performative, habitual, running commentary, demonstration, staged action, historic narrative, or a timeless synopsis. Avoid flagging these constructions solely because the event is not instantaneous.
- A caption can combine present tense with a past-time adjunct while describing a fixed visual record. Describing a text/artwork's current content differs from narrating its dated creation.
