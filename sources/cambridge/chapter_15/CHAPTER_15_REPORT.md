## Source-specific cautions
- Coordination is **non-headed**. Coordinates have equal syntactic status, not head/dependent functions; the complete construction has its own coordination category.
- Replacement of the whole by one coordinate and order-reversal are useful **normal** diagnostics, not absolute requirements; the printed p. 1275 footnote points to joint coordination in §1.3.2 as a counterexample.
- Mixed category coordination is allowed, including NP/PP coordination where syntactic functions are compatible.
- Binary vs multiple, syndetic vs asyndetic, simple-syndetic vs polysyndetic, and correlative vs non-correlative are independent grammatical distinctions.
- Medial marking is uniform **within a single unlayered multiple syndetic coordination**. Do not automatically reject sequences admitting a layered parse; §1.2 is a deferred dependency.
- The initial correlative `both`/`either` is classified as a determinative, whereas `and`/`or` is the coordinator.
- The coordinator forms a syntactic unit with the **following** coordinate, as shown by movable second coordinates, sentence-initial coordinators, and punctuation/prosodic breaks before coordinators.
- A **bare coordinate** is distinguished from an **expanded coordinate** containing a coordinator and/or modifiers. Figure [9] on p. 1278 includes markers and adverbial modifiers; no source diagram is copied.
