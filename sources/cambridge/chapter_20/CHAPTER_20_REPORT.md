## Coverage and interpretation safeguards
- **Punctuation indicator** is broader than a punctuation mark: it includes segmental marks and spaces and non-segmental alterations of existing letters.
- **Spaces are segmental**. They occupy positions in the written sequence, unlike typographic emphasis or case changes.
- An initial sentence capital differs structurally from the terminal full stop, question mark or exclamation mark; this is a definition, not a full set of enforcement rules.
- The apostrophe and ordinary hyphen are **normally word-internal**. This subsection does not extract their detailed placement conditions.
- Most diacritics count as **spelling, not punctuation**. The diaeresis in certain American forms such as coöperate is an explicitly borderline exception noted on p.1725.
- Quotes and parentheses may encompass multiple sentences, while paragraph breaks fall within punctuation when represented by a new line, usually with indentation.
- Chapters, sections and heading hierarchy **do not** fall within the punctuation domain merely because typography is used. Italics can be punctuation when indicating a title or quotation but layout-only when indicating heading rank.
- The source section supplies no new universal punctuation-error constraints, so **7 classifications and 1 descriptive generalisation** were extracted instead.
- The repository has no dedicated punctuation domain. New records are in `grammar/rules/other.json`, with the domain question logged as a limitation; no schema modification or protocol version bump.
