## Key distinctions and safeguards
- Infinitivals are non-finite despite their plain-form verb, unlike finite imperatives and subjunctives; supportive do is excluded from infinitival negation.
- The three main non-finite form-types are infinitival, gerund-participial and past-participial. Verbless clauses are not a fourth verb-inflectional form-type.
- Infinitivals allow perfect have, progressive be and passive be. Gerund-participials allow perfect have and passive be but not **productive progressive being + -ing** in established Present-day English. The source's rare casual attestations are recorded as a limitation, not quietly discarded.
- Past participles used in perfect and passive constructions have different internal auxiliary possibilities.
- Subjectless non-finite clauses do not need an overt subject; the constituent can be a clause with VP head. Attributive VPs before NP heads are an explicit structural exception.
- Hollow clauses lack an understood **non-subject**, normally recovered from matrix material. An omitted subject is not necessarily recoverable from the matrix.
- Interrogative and relative to-infinitivals exist, but their detailed behaviour is reserved by the source for Cambridge Chapters 11 §5.3.5 and 12 §5.
- Non-finite clauses function as complements within several categories or as modifiers and supplements; constructions that convey a purpose need not share the same syntactic function.
