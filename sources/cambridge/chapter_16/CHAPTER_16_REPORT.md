## Important safeguards
- The existing `CLS-047` statement that packaging does not alter truth conditions required qualification. Most straightforward examples preserve truth conditions and illocutionary force, but they need not preserve truth conditions when `many` and negation change relative scope.
- Some noncanonical versions have no acceptable basic alternative, for syntactic or pragmatic reasons.
- Nine information-packaging constructions are listed: preposing, postposing, subject–dependent inversion, existential, extraposition, left dislocation, right dislocation, cleft and passive.
- Subject–dependent inversion must not be conflated with subject–auxiliary inversion (`CLS-013`).
- Process terminology indicates a comparison of structure, not a literal derivational operation.
- Reordering (first three constructions) differs from realignment of meaning and syntactic functions (remaining six).
- Multiple information-packaging types can coexist, but the described existential and subject-extraposition constructions cannot coexist due to structural prerequisites.
- The source reserves certain labels as construction labels (e.g. preposing), and others as both clause and construction labels (passive, cleft, existential).
- None of the new classifications or descriptive generalisations is an automatic grammar-error constraint.
